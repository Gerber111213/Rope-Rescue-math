    River Features & Hazards Glossary

    Eddy Safe slackwater zone behind obstacles where water flows upstream; vital staging.
    Pillow Water piling up on upstream side of boulders, creating a hydraulic cushion.
    Hydraulic (Hole) Water plunging over a ledge and recirculating upstream. Keeper holes trap swimmers.
    Undercut Rocks or banks hollowed out underneath by current; extreme entrapment risk.
    Strainer Obstructions (trees, fences, grates) that let water pass while trapping solid objects and bodies.

    Standard Whistle Commands

    • 1 Blast (Short): Stop / Look / Attention.
    • 2 Blasts (Short): Forward / Move / Paddle on.
    • 3 Blasts (Short): Back Up / Reverse.
    • Long Continuous Blast: 🚨 EMERGENCY / SOS

    The Rescue Progression

    • Reach: Reach with voice or tools from shore.
    • Throw: Deploy throw bag to conscious swimmer.
    • Row: Use watercraft to effect rescue.
    • Go: In-water contact rescue by trained technician. Last resort.

    Advanced Structural Collapse & Shoring

    T-Stops, Double T-Stops, flying rakers, standard rakers, wood member failure points, and Paratech metal struts.

    Advanced Shoring Configurations: T-Stops & Double T-Stops

    T-Stop Shoring Installed against leaning or bulging masonry/concrete walls to prevent inward collapse. Combines a vertical wall plate, header, and diagonal bracing struts at a 45° to 60° angle.
    Double T-Stop Shoring Features dual vertical posts tied with plywood gussets and cross-bracing. Used for high-load wall stabilization where single posts risk buckling under eccentric seismic or lateral loads.
    Standard Raker Systems Applied to unsupported exterior walls. Includes wall plate, shore beam (compression member), sole plate, and diagonal brace tied at a 45° angle.
    Flying Raker Suspended raker system used when debris or obstacles prevent ground-level sole plate placement near the base of an unstable wall.

    Surface Water Rescue, Boat Handling & Body Recovery

    Search grid patterns, body recovery protocols, boat handling in current, and rescuer personal boating safety.

    Boat Handling & Navigation in Moving Water

    • Ferry Angle (Crossing Current): To cross a river in a motorized or paddle watercraft, angle the bow upstream at 45° to the current vector. The vector force pushes the boat across safely. Never point straight across (perpendicular) or the current will swamp the transom.
    • Entering & Exiting Eddies: Always cross the eddy line with the bow pointed upstream at a sharp angle (45° to 60°) to prevent "catch-and-flip" where current grabs the downstream tube/gunwale.
    • Motorized Boat Rescue Approach: Approach victims from downstream, motoring up into the eddy or backing down slowly with the stern facing away from obstacles. Maintain prop clearance from lines and swimmers.

    Rescuer Boating Safety & Personal PPE

    • Quick-Release Harness: All boat operators and crew in moving water must wear Type V rescue PFDs equipped with a quick-release chest harness and cowtail. Never tether yourself to a boat in moving water without an instant-release mechanism (entrapment hazard).
    • Flip Recovery: Crew members must practice immediate boat righting and defensive swimming posture (feet up, pointing downstream, toes surface-level) if watercraft capsizes.

    Surface Water Search Patterns & Body Recovery Protocols

    Expanding Square Search Best when Last Known Position (LKP) is highly accurate. Expands outward in concentric square legs.
    Parallel Track Search Used for broad water bodies. Multiple watercraft sweep in synchronized parallel lines spaced by visibility limits.
    Subsurface Body Recovery Bodies typically sink in cold water, settling downstream in catch points (eddies, bridge piers, log jams, deep scour holes). Recovery teams utilize sector searches and underwater sonar / probing.

    To purge a confined space safely, calculate total volume ($V = \pi r^2 h$ or length $\times$ width $\times$ height). Standard protocol requires a minimum of **complete air changes** prior to entry.

    • Blower Placement: Position fresh air blowers upwind and away from exhaust fumes or vehicle tailpipes.
    • Retrieval Tripod Systems: Rated for personnel winching (typically 500 lbs working load limit) with independent mechanical backup belay lines.

    Ice Rescue Tactics: Slot Cutting & Load Distribution

    • Load Distribution Platforms: Deploy rescue boards, ladders, or inflated pontoon boats across weak ice to spread rescuer weight over a large surface area, preventing punch-through.
    • Slot Cutting (Vehicle Recovery): When a vehicle breaks through thick ice, responders cut an extraction slot in the ice downstream or away from the vehicle to allow the chassis to be pulled back out onto solid sheets without breaking further.
    • Ice Awls / Picks Self-Rescue: Victims attempting self-rescue must kick horizontally while driving ice awls into solid ice above the lip, pulling forward rather than vertical vertical climbing.

    Thermal Survival & Cold Water Immersion Curves

    Water temperatures near 0°C to 2°C cause cold shock within 1–3 minutes (gasp reflex, hyperventilation) followed by functional impairment within 30 minutes (swim failure). However, mammalian dive reflex in freezing water can preserve central neurological function longer in select cases. Rescuers must treat all cold-water immersion cardiac arrests aggressively with resuscitation upon extraction.

    Specialized Field Medical Care Plans

    Canadian wilderness medical protocols for hypothermia, hyperthermia, drowning, burns, harness hand, and pain management.

    1. Hypothermia Care Plan

    • Gentle Handling (Critical): Handle patients extremely gently. Rough movement can trigger "after-drop" (cold peripheral blood rushing to the core), causing fatal ventricular fibrillation.
    • Vapor Barrier & Insulation: Remove wet garments carefully. Wrap in wool/down blankets with an external vapor-barrier wrap (tarp/plastic) to block wind and convective heat loss.
    • Warm Fluids: If conscious and swallowing reflex is intact, administer warm, sweet liquids. Avoid alcohol and caffeine.

    2. Hyperthermia Care Plan

    • Active Cooling: Move patient immediately to shade or air-conditioned/sheltered vehicle. Remove heavy gear and outer clothing.
    • Evaporative & Ice Cooling: Apply ice packs to high-flow vascular areas (groin, axillae/armpits, neck). Mist skin with tepid water while fanning aggressively.
    • Hydration: If alert, administer oral electrolyte solutions in small, frequent sips. Evacuate immediately if mental status is altered (heat stroke).

    3. Drowning Care Plan

    • Airway & Breathing (ABCs): Clear airway immediately. Initiate rescue breathing / CPR instantly if pulseless or apneic. Do not waste time attempting to "drain" water from lungs.
    • Spinal Precautions: Assume cervical spine injury if mechanism involves diving into shallow water or striking submerged obstacles.
    • Oxygen & Rewarming: Administer high-flow supplemental oxygen if available and initiate concurrent hypothermia insulation protocols.

    4. Burns Care Plan

    • Stop the Burning Process: Cool thermal burns with clean, cool (not freezing) water for 10–20 minutes. Remove constrictive jewelry/rings before swelling begins.
    • Sterile Dressings: Cover burn areas loosely with dry, sterile, non-adherent dressings. Never apply ointments, butter, or ice directly to severe burns.
    • Inhalation Assessment: Monitor closely for facial burns, singed nasal hairs, or hoarseness indicating airway edema.

    5. Harness Hand / Suspension Trauma (Orthostatic Intolerance) Care Plan

    • Immediate Relief: Rescue suspended patient from vertical harness immediately. Deploy anti-trauma foot loops or leg stirrups if hanging is prolonged.
    • Post-Rescue Position (Crucial): Once rescued, keep patient in a sitting or semi-recumbent position for at least 30 minutes. Never lay a suspension trauma patient flat on their back instantly, as pooled venous blood rushing to the heart can cause cardiac overload and sudden death.

    6. Pain Management Care Plan

    • Immobilization & Splinting: Prioritize proper anatomical splinting and traction splints to reduce muscular spasm and fracture site movement.
    • Triage & Vital Monitoring: Assess pain via standard scales (1–10). Monitor vital signs continuously before administering authorized field analgesics or entonox (nitrous oxide) per provincial paramedic scope of practice.
