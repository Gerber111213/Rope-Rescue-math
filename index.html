    Medical Priorities & Drowning Care Flow Chart

    Immediate triage protocol for pulled swift water victims. For advanced procedures, reference the Remote Medical Module (Drowning Care).

    Step 1: Scene Safety & Extraction — Ensure victim is secure from current and suspect spinal trauma if mechanism of injury involves rocks or debris.
    Step 2: Airway & Breathing (ABCs) — Clear airway of water/debris. Initiate rescue breaths/CPR immediately if pulseless/apneic. Cold-water submersion reflex can prolong survivability.
    Step 3: Circulation & Hypothermia — Remove wet garments, insulate victim to prevent after-drop, and monitor core temperature.
    Step 4: CASEVAC / Transport — Rapid transfer to definitive emergency medical care.

    International Scale of River Difficulty

    ClassDescription & Characteristics
    Class IFast-moving water with small riffles and few obstruction points. Clear and easy self-rescue.
    Class IIStraightforward rapids with wide channels and clear passages. Occasional maneuvering required.
    Class IIIModerate, irregular waves, complex maneuvers, and narrow passages. May require scouting for unexperienced groups.
    Class IVIntense, powerful rapids requiring precise boat handling and high-consequence rescue profiles.
    Class VExtremely long, obstructed, or violent rapids with massive drops, unavoidable hydraulics, and high hazard levels.
    Class VIUnrunnable / Navigability limits. Extreme risk to life; rescue attempted only from shore with high-line systems.

    River Features & Hazards Glossary

    Eddy Safe slackwater zone behind rocks/banks where water flows upstream; vital for staging and catch points.
    Pillow Water piling up on the upstream side of a large obstacle, creating a hydraulic cushion and a downstream wave.
    Hydraulic (Hole) Water plunging over a ledge and recirculating upstream. Keeper holes trap objects and swimmers.
    Undercut Rocks or banks hollowed out underneath by current; extreme entrapment risk.
    Strainer Obstructions (trees, fences, grates) that let water pass while trapping solid objects and bodies.

    Standard Whistle Commands

    • 1 Blast (Short): Stop / Look / Attention.
    • 2 Blasts (Short): Forward / Move / Paddle on.
    • 3 Blasts (Short): Back Up / Reverse.
    • Long Continuous Blast: 🚨 EMERGENCY / SOS (All units stop and look to command).

    The Rescue Progression ("Reach, Throw, Row, Go")

    • Reach (Voice & Extension): Reach with your voice or tools (poles, branches) from shore. Lowest risk.
    • Throw: Deploy a throw bag to a conscious swimmer.
    • Row: Use watercraft (rafts, kayaks, motorized boats) to effect rescue.
    • Go: In-water contact rescue by trained technician. Highest risk; last resort.

    Key NFPA Standards for Swift Water Rescue

    • NFPA 1006: Standard for Technical Rescuer Professional Qualifications (Defines Awareness, Operations, and Technician levels for water rescue).
    • NFPA 1670: Standard for Operations and Training for Technical Search and Rescue Incidents (Organizational responsibilities & safety).
    • NFPA 1620: Standard for Pre-Incident Planning (Covers water hazards and local geography planning).
    • NFPA 1500: Standard on Occupational Safety, Health, and Wellness Program.
    • NFPA 1952: Standard on Protective Ensembles for Water Rescues.
