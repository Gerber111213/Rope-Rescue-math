    Knots & Hitches Tying Pictographs

    Standalone knot & hitch visual guide showing step-by-step formation (Blue = standing/primary, Yellow = working/wrap, Green = dressed/locked).

    Figure-8 on a Bight ~75–80% Strength

    Primary anchor & rigging loop. High strength retention and easy untying after load.

    Step 1: Bight
    Form a clean bight
    Step 2: Full Turn
    360° twist around stem
    Step 3: Tuck & Dress
    Pass through eye & dress

    Bowline + Backup ~70–75% Strength

    Resists jamming under heavy tension. Always add a stopper/backup on the tail.

    Step 1: Overhand Loop
    Form small loop in line
    Step 2: Rabbit Hole
    Up through eye, around tree
    Step 3: Back Down
    Back down eye & snug

    Alpine Butterfly Loop ~75–80% Strength

    Midline non-slip loop that can be loaded in any direction or in-line.

    Step 1: Two Wraps
    Wrap two turns on hand
    Step 2: Fold Across
    Bring left coil over right
    Step 3: Pull & Dress
    Pull loop through & dress

    Prusik Friction Hitch ~70–80% Strength

    Slides when unloaded, grips when weighted. Use cord ~2–4mm thinner than host rope.

    Step 1: Girth Hitch
    Loop around host rope
    Step 2: Extra Coils
    2–3 wraps inside bight
    Step 3: Set Coils
    Align coils neatly & test

    Double Fisherman's Bend ~75–85% Strength

    Strong bend for joining two ends or making prusik/anchor loops. Snugs tight under load.

    Step 1: Parallel Ends
    Lay tails side-by-side
    Step 2: Double Wraps
    2 turns around opposing line
    Step 3: Lock Together
    cinch both knots tight

    Barrel Knot (Stopper) ~65–70% Strength

    Bulky stopper knot for tail security or single-line termination.

    Step 1: Bight & Tail
    Bring tail over standing line
    Step 2: Coil Turns
    Wrap turns toward bight
    Step 3: Tuck & Dress
    Pass through & cinch

    Critical Swiftwater Tactics

    Foot Entrapment Protocols Avoid standing in fast water above knee depth. Adopt defensive swimming posture (on back, feet up and downstream) to negotiate obstacles and eddy out.
    Live-Bait Tether Safety Use an approved quick-release system with a dedicated tender watching the swimmer continuously and ready to release immediately if pinned or entrapped.
    Downstream Safety Cell Position spotters, throw bags, and backup rescue assets downstream of the hazard to intercept swimmers or gear swept past the primary site.

    Standard Whistle Commands

    • 1 Short Blast: Stop / Look / Attention
    • 2 Short Blasts: Forward / Move downstream or across
    • 3 Short Blasts: Back up / Reverse
    • Long Continuous Blast: 🚨 Emergency / Man Down / Abort

    Rescue Progression

    • 1. Reach: Verbal guidance or pole/line from shore without entering water.
    • 2. Throw: Throw bag to conscious victim.
    • 3. Row: Watercraft approach/rescue.
    • 4. Go: Tethered/un-tethered technician entry — highest risk option.

    EV / Hybrid High-Voltage Protocols

    • High-Voltage Orange Cables: Never cut, crush, or pierce bright orange HV cabling/conduit.
    • 12V System & Isolation: Follow manufacturer emergency response guide for 12V disconnect/service plug procedures where safe and appropriate.
    • Thermal Runaway: Watch for delayed or repeated re-ignition; use large volumes of water for cooling and monitor with thermal imaging.

    Heavy Bus / Coach Stabilization

    Stabilize near suspension/frame/axles first and chock wheels before lifting or prying heavy compartments.

    Advanced Structural Collapse & Shoring

    Wall assessment, strut load considerations, and breaching timelines.

    Wall Assessment Matrix

    Structure TypePrimary HazardsTypical Shoring Approach
    Unreinforced Masonry (URM)Out-of-plane wall failure, falling brick/debrisRakers, T-stops, or wall bracing per structural assessment.
    Tilt-Up ConcretePanel connection/brace failure, sudden inward/outward movementHeavy rakers/struts with positive mechanical locking.
    Wood FrameSagging floors/roofs, shifting load paths, utilitiesPost shores, laced posts, and lateral bracing.

    Strut & Breaching Reference

    • Axial vs. Off-Axis Loads: Keep struts aligned axially and locked; off-axis angles reduce capacity quickly.
    • Breaching Benchmarks: Rotary hammer stitch drilling in concrete is slow (~1–2 in/min depending on aggregate/rebar); rebar/timber cutting is fast (~5–15 sec per member).

    Surface Water & Boat Operations

    Current navigation, night/decon procedures, and aviation hoist signals.

    Boat Handling in Current

    • Ferry Angle: Angle bow upstream at ~45° to use current thrust to cross without turning broadside.
    • Eddy Lines: Commit cleanly across eddy boundaries with proper bow angle to avoid broaching/capsizing.

    Night Ops, Decon & Hoist Signals

    Night / Low Visibility Use high-vis markers, scene lighting, thermal imaging, and clear redundant voice/whistle commands.
    Decontamination Perform gross decon and PPE doffing/disinfection after exposure to sewage, agricultural runoff, or hazardous runoff.
    Hoist Signals Arms raised in a "Y" = Ready/Yes; arms down/crossed = Do Not Hoist/Cancel. Secure loose gear in rotor wash.

    Confined Space Rescue

    Entry controls, atmospheric monitoring thresholds, and rapid extraction priorities.

    Pre-Entry & Rescue Readiness

    • Energy Isolation (LOTO): Lock out/tag out electrical, mechanical, hydraulic, and pneumatic energy sources.
    • Continuous Gas Monitoring: Test and monitor O2, LEL, H2S, and CO from top to bottom before and during entry.
    • Standby Rescue: Maintain dedicated rescue personnel and retrieval/breathing gear ready prior to entry.

    Comprehensive Ice Rescue

    Ice thickness reference, degradation factors, and spaced tether chains.

    Clear Solid Ice Load Guidelines

    Thickness (Clear Blue/Black Ice)General Load GuidanceNotes
    < 2 inchesStay offUnsafe for personnel.
    2–3 inchesSingle person (caution)Flotation suit & belay recommended.
    4 inches~200 lbs single rescuerMinimum threshold for tethered rescue entry.
    5 inchesGroup / sled (spaced)Maintain spacing (~15 ft+) between rescuers.
    8+ inchesLight vehicle / ATVVerify local ice conditions and thickness across path.

    Degradation & Tether Chain Rules

    • Thermal / Spring Degradation: Warm temperatures, sun, currents, or snow insulation weaken ice regardless of thickness.
    • Spaced Rescuer Chain: Space tethered rescuers apart (~15 ft+) so a single breakthrough does not pull multiple rescuers in.

    Hemorrhage Control Kit Contents

    Windlass Tourniquets Severe extremity arterial bleeding control.
    Hemostatic Gauze Wound packing for deep/junctional bleeding with direct pressure.
    Compressed / Rolled Gauze & Bandages Pressure dressings and securement.
    Chest Seals Vented/non-vented seals for open chest wounds.
    Thermal Blanket & Trauma Shears Hypothermia prevention and clothing access.

    START Triage & WMA Wilderness Care

    • BLACK: Deceased / apneic after airway positioning.
    • RED: Immediate — RR >30, delayed perfusion/CRT, or cannot follow simple commands.
    • YELLOW: Delayed — serious injuries but stable enough to wait.
    • GREEN: Minor / walking wounded.
    • WMA Wilderness Approach: Long-evacuation patient monitoring, serial vitals, insulation, and hydration/nutrition planning.

    Alberta Scope Overview (SFA to ACP)

    LevelTypical Capabilities
    Standard First Aid (SFA)CPR/AED, bleeding control, splinting, basic assessment.
    AFA / OFRSupplemental O2, airway adjuncts, wound packing, tourniquets, spinal motion restriction.
    Primary Care Paramedic (PCP)Supraglottic airway, IV access, authorized symptom-relief medications.
    Advanced Care Paramedic (ACP)Advanced airway/intubation, IV/IO medications, advanced resuscitation/procedures per protocols.

    Core Care Plans

    • Hypothermia: Handle gently, insulate from ground/wind, use vapor barrier, rewarm per protocol.
    • Hyperthermia: Move to shade/cool environment, active cooling, rehydrate if alert.
    • Drowning: Airway/ventilation/CPR priority, treat hypothermia and hypoxia.
    • Burns: Cool water cooling (avoid ice), clean dry sterile dressing, prevent hypothermia.
    • Suspension Trauma ("Harness Hang"): Gentle extrication, avoid sudden flat positioning if symptomatic, monitor closely.
    • Pain Management: Splint/immobilize, monitor vitals, administer authorized analgesia per scope.
