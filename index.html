    Critical Swiftwater Tactics: Entrapment, Live-Bait & Safety Cells

    Foot Entrapment Protocols Never attempt to stand up in moving water faster than knee-deep. Current pressure pins legs instantly. Swimmers must float defensively on their back feet up, angle toward shore, and roll over banks.
    Live-Bait Tether Safety Mechanics Rescuer is tethered to a quick-release harness on shore or via a tension diagonal. The handler must maintain absolute visual contact and be ready to deploy the instant-release buckle if the rescuer gets swept underwater or snagged.
    Downstream Safety Cell Setup Positioned below any technical rescue or rapid hazard. Equipped with throw bags, rescue watercraft, and backup tethered swimmers to intercept victims who slip past primary operational zones.

    Technical Rescue River Difficulty Matrix

    ClassWater Velocity & WavesRescueability & Operational Profile
    Class I1–3 fps (Fast riffles, waves < 1 ft)High (Self-Rescue / Reach): Easy self-rescue. Standard PFDs sufficient.
    Class II3–6 fps (Straightforward rapids, waves 1–2 ft)Moderate (Throw Bag): Swimmers can catch eddies easily. Effective throw bag zone.
    Class III6–10 fps (Irregular waves, waves 2–4 ft)Challenging (Tension / Boat): Strainers & cold water present severe hazards. High-side boats required.
    Class IV10–15 fps (Powerful rapids, waves 4–6 ft)High Consequence (Technician Only): Recirculating hydraulics. Live-bait rescues and advanced boat handling required.
    Class V15–20+ fps (Violent drops, waves 6+ ft)Extreme / Marginal: High fatality rate for swimmers. In-water contact rescue is extremely high risk.
    Class VIUnpredictable / Flood StageZero In-Water Entry: Unrunnable catastrophic debris fields. Shore or aerial recovery only.

    Standard Whistle Commands

    • 1 Blast (Short): Stop / Look / Attention.
    • 2 Blasts (Short): Forward / Move / Paddle on.
    • 3 Blasts (Short): Back Up / Reverse.
    • Long Continuous Blast: 🚨 EMERGENCY / SOS

    The Rescue Progression

    • Reach: Reach with voice or tools from shore.
    • Throw: Deploy throw bag to conscious swimmer.
    • Row: Use watercraft to effect rescue.
    • Go: In-water contact rescue by trained technician. Last resort.

    Alternative Fuel Vehicle (EV/Hybrid) & High-Voltage Protocols

    • High-Voltage Orange Cables: Never cut, pinch, or crush bright orange HV cables. They carry up to 400V–800V DC.
    • First Responder Disconnects: Locate 12V auxiliary battery in hood or trunk and cut/disconnect negative terminal first to deactivate contactors.
    • Lithium-Ion Thermal Runaway: Damaged battery packs can reignite hours later. Apply massive water volumes (up to 3,000+ gallons) for cooling and monitor with thermal imaging cameras.

    Heavy Transit Bus Stabilization & Graphics

    Transit and highway motor coaches feature heavy rear-engine weight bias and large baggage compartments.

    • Cribbing & Strut Placement: Place heavy 6x6 wood cribbing and pneumatic struts directly under front axle and rear wheel wells prior to lower compartment rescue operations.

    Paratech Metal Rescue Strut Load Capacity Matrix

    • Short / Medium / Long Shores: Safe working load capacity ranges from 10,000 to 20,000 lbs depending on extension length.
    • Off-Axis Loading Rule: Every 5 degrees of off-axis angular load reduces strut working capacity by approximately 10%. Always pin struts securely.

    Breaching & Breaking Tool Timelines

    • Rotary Hammer Drills (Concrete/Masonry): Core drilling/stitch drilling at ~1 to 2 inches per minute in reinforced concrete.
    • Gas/Battery Cut-Off Saws (Steel Rebar / Wood): Cuts standard 1-inch rebar in 10-15 seconds; structural timber 4x4 in 5-10 seconds.
    • Hydraulic Breakers / Combi Tools: Rapid displacement of door frames, structural framing, and vehicle pillars in under 30 seconds.

    Surface Water Rescue, Boat Handling & Specialized Ops

    Night search tactics, decontamination protocols, and helicopter hoist rescue signals.

    Boat Handling & Navigation in Moving Water

    • Ferry Angle (Crossing Current): Angle bow upstream at 45° to the current vector. Vector force pushes boat across safely. Never point straight across.
    • Entering & Exiting Eddies: Cross eddy line with bow pointed upstream at 45° to 60° to prevent "catch-and-flip".

    Night Operations, Decontamination & Helicopter Hoist Signals

    Night / Low-Light Operations Utilize waterproof tactical lighting, thermal imaging drones/cameras, and acoustic hailing systems. Maintain strict verbal and whistle command redundancy.
    Contaminated Water Decon Post-rescue decontamination using disinfectant rinses and PPE disposal for responders exposed to raw sewage, agricultural runoff, or industrial chemical spills.
    Helicopter Hoist Signals Arms raised in "Y" means "Yes / Ready for Hoist". Arms extended down in "N" means "No / Do Not Hoist". Keep low profile during rotor-wash approach.

    Confined Space Rescue Command Hub

    Permit checklists, atmospheric monitoring limits, 4-minute extraction timelines, and tripod systems.

    Permit-Required Confined Space Checklist

    • Lockout / Tagout (LOTO): Verify zero energy state for electrical, hydraulic, and pneumatic lines entering vessel.
    • Continuous Atmospheric Monitoring: Calibrate and test sensors for O2, LEL, H2S, and CO prior to and during entry.
    • Standby Rescue Team: Qualified rescue team stationed outside portal with SCBA and retrieval gear ready before entry is authorized.

    IDLH & Acceptable Atmospheric Parameters (OHS Standards)

    ParameterNormal Safe RangeAction Threshold / IDLH Limit
    Oxygen (O2)20.8% - 21.0%< 19.5% (Deficient) or > 23.5% (Enriched - Fire Hazard)
    Flammable Vapors (LEL)0% LEL> 10% LEL requires immediate evacuation and ventilation
    Hydrogen Sulfide (H2S)0 ppm> 10 ppm (Action) / 100 ppm (IDLH - instant paralysis)
    Carbon Monoxide (CO)0 ppm> 25 ppm (Action) / 1200 ppm (IDLH threshold)

    Critical 4-Minute Extraction Window

    In an oxygen-deficient or toxic atmosphere (e.g., high H2S), brain hypoxia leads to irreversible damage within 4 to 6 minutes. Rescue teams must prioritize immediate mechanical winch retrieval over complex medical treatment inside the hazard zone.

    Ice Strength Degradation Factors & Multi-Rescuer Chains

    • Rotten Spring Ice: Sun-warmed ice loses structural strength rapidly even if thickness appears substantial. Reduce rated capacities by 50% in spring conditions.
    • Multi-Rescuer Tethered Chain Protocol: For heavy or unresponsive victims, deploy rescuers in a distributed chain connected by webbing lines separated by at least 15 feet to prevent multiple team members from punching through the same breakthrough hole.

    Hemostatic Gauze (e.g., QuikClot) Z-folded gauze impregnated with kaolin for rapid clotting on junctional/wound bleeding.

    Mass-Casualty Triage (START Algorithm) & WMA Wilderness Protocols

    • BLACK (Expectant / Deceased): Apneic after manual airway repositioning.
    • RED (Immediate): Respirations >30, CRT >2 sec, or unable to follow simple commands.
    • YELLOW (Delayed): Serious injuries but stable; does not fit immediate criteria.
    • GREEN (Minor / Walking Wounded): Ambulatory minor injuries.
    • WMA International Wilderness Protocols: Extended patient assessments for prolonged backcountry evacuations where definitive care exceeds 2–24 hours. Focus on serial vitals tracking and hypothermia prevention.

    Core Care Plans (Hypothermia, Hyperthermia, Drowning, Burns, Harness Hand, Pain)

    • Hypothermia: Gentle handling to prevent after-drop ventricular fibrillation; vapor-barrier wrap.
    • Hyperthermia: Active cooling with ice packs to groin/axillae; electrolyte rehydration.
    • Drowning: Immediate airway clearance and CPR; treat concurrent hypothermia.
    • Burns: Cool water irrigation (10-20 min); sterile dry dressings; no ointments or ice.
    • Harness Hand / Suspension Trauma: Immediate vertical rescue; keep patient in semi-recumbent sitting position post-rescue (never lay flat instantly).
    • Pain Management: Anatomical splinting, vitals monitoring, and authorized analgesic administration per Alberta EMS scope.
